## Question 4. Digital inclusion: older citizens and the Digital Post mandate

**The group and the policy.** The mandate falls most heavily on the oldest
citizens, whose only relief is statutory exemption from Digital Post.
Digitaliseringsstyrelsen [[DG1:y]] reports that nearly one citizen in five aged
75 to 84 holds a formal exemption, rising to roughly one in three among those 85
and over. Those are 2022 figures against a 2026 rate below, so the two are
complementary rather than points on a trend.

**Effectiveness, and what the headline conceals.** The measure on which Denmark
ranks first is the one least able to detect this problem. The Eurostat survey
behind the 98.5% asks only whether a person interacted online at all, once, in
twelve months: it records reach, and is silent on whether the attempt succeeded
or was made independently ([[F12]]). In the first quarter of 2026, 4.7% of citizens aged 15
and over, or 238,479 people, were exempt, the narrowest available measure of the
problem; [[F4]] sets it against four wider estimates that are not comparable
because measure, year and denominator all differ, and [[AP:exclusion]]
reconciles them. The European Commission [[EC1:y]] reports that 6.5% of Danes do
not use digital public services and 16.5% report difficulty using them, while
Digitaliseringsstyrelsen [[DG2:y]] and Justitia [[JU1:y]] put the widest
estimates at about a quarter of adults. The counts are clearer than the
percentages: 16.5% of 6,025,603 [[DST2]] is roughly 994,000 against 238,479
exempt, so on the order of three-quarters of a million Danes struggle with a
compulsory system while holding no standing outside it.

**The barrier is administrative, not a skills deficit.** Denmark's weakest age
band, those aged 55 to 74, reaches 67.81% with at least basic digital skills
against an EU average of 42.60%, and leads the Union in all three bands
([[F10]]). The mandate is calibrated above the bottom of its own distribution,
and the exemption criteria are drawn more narrowly than the difficulty they
relieve.

**Recommendation.** The exemption criteria should be widened to admit documented
difficulty rather than only the listed statutory grounds. Denmark has already
solved the adjacent problem: Folketinget [[LOV1:y]] provided that from June 2023
an exempt citizen is entitled, on request, to a non-digital alternative to any
mandatory self-service solution. That decoupled the channel from the obligation
but left the qualifying criteria untouched, because the right runs only to those
already exempt. The Conseil d'État [[CE1:y]] and the Belgian Cour
constitutionnelle [[BE1:y]] have since attached the entitlement to the difficulty
rather than to a prior status ([[AP:comparative_law]]).

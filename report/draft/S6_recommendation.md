## 6. Recommendation and limitations

This report opened on a projected saving of roughly DKK 1,000 million a year. Of
that, Rigsrevisionen [[RR1:y]] could verify DKK 450 million in postage, paper and
envelopes. The wage and overhead component was never substantiated, and the study intended
to settle the question was abandoned when the municipalities declined to take
part. Denmark compels its adult population on the basis of a business case that
remains 55% unaudited.

Two measures follow. First, the exemption criteria in *Lov om Offentlig Digital
Post* should be widened to admit documented difficulty. Denmark has already
addressed the adjacent problem, since Folketinget [[LOV1:y]] provided that from
June 2023 an exempt citizen is entitled, on request, to a non-digital alternative
to any mandatory self-service solution. That reform decoupled the channel from the
obligation but left the qualifying criteria untouched, because the right runs only
to those already exempt. Section 4 measured the gap: roughly three-quarters of a
million people report the difficulty the 2023 Act relieves and cannot obtain it. The Conseil d'État
[[CE1:y]] and the Belgian Cour constitutionnelle [[BE1:y]] have since attached the
entitlement to the difficulty rather than to a prior status (Appendix G).

Second, SMV:Digital should be redirected toward the complementary capability,
specifically data integration and specialist skills, that Section 3 identified as
the binding constraint. Its effect is better evidenced than that of most comparable programmes, since
Digitaliseringsstyrelsen [[DG5:y]] matches recipients to comparable firms in the
Danmarks Statistik registers and finds revenue growth five percentage points
higher. Matching, however, addresses selection on what the registers record,
namely sector, size, age and prior trajectory, not on what they do not. For a
voluntary scheme that residual is the substantive problem, because a manager
ambitious enough to apply is also ambitious enough to grow unaided. The next pool opens on 26 October 2026 [[SMV1]], and the scheme's
eligibility scoring already produces a threshold capable of identifying the effect
directly, as Appendix F specifies.

Both measures follow from a single reading of the evidence: Denmark built shared
infrastructure and left the questions of who could use it, and who could build on
it, to a capability it never distributed. The evidence has limits, set out in
Appendices C to G; chief among them is that Section 2 establishes association,
not cause.
